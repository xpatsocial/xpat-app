# SVP Verification Report: User Research & Engagement Psychology Claims

**Prepared by:** SVP of Research & Intelligence
**Date:** April 8, 2026
**Scope:** Independent verification of claims in four VP-level research reports
**Verdict:** Mixed. Most demographic data checks out. Several engagement statistics are misattributed, conflated, or unverifiable. Two claims are outright wrong.

---

## Methodology

Every specific data point flagged by the CEO was independently verified via primary sources (company blogs, investor filings, peer-reviewed journals, industry benchmark reports). Where a claim could not be traced to a credible primary source, it is marked UNVERIFIABLE. Where the original report stated a number incorrectly or misattributed a source, it is marked CORRECTED.

---

## VERIFIED (Claim substantially accurate)

### 1. "43M+ digital nomads worldwide"
- **Verdict: VERIFIED**
- Multiple 2025-2026 sources converge on 40-45 million. DemandSage (2026) cites 43 million. The range of 43-45M stated in the persona report is defensible, though the lower bound of the full range across sources is closer to 40M.
- The "147% increase since 2019" figure is close; MBO Partners reports 153% increase since 2019 for US nomads specifically. The global figure is less precisely tracked.
- Sources: [DemandSage 2026](https://www.demandsage.com/digital-nomads-statistics/), [MBO Partners 2025](https://www.mbopartners.com/state-of-independence/digital-nomads/)

### 2. "Facebook's magic number was 7 friends in 10 days"
- **Verdict: VERIFIED**
- This is directly attributable to Chamath Palihapitiya at the 2012 Growth Hackers Conference. He stated this was Facebook's "single sole focus" and that users who reached 7 friends within 10 days showed dramatically reduced churn. Well-documented across multiple primary and secondary sources.
- Sources: [Chamath at Growth Hackers Conference](https://ryangum.com/chamath-palihapitiya-how-we-put-facebook-on-the-path-to-1-billion-users/), [Mode Blog](https://mode.com/blog/facebook-aha-moment-simpler-than-you-think/)

### 3. "Slack's magic number was 2,000 messages"
- **Verdict: VERIFIED with IMPORTANT NUANCE**
- Stewart Butterfield confirmed this in a First Round Review interview. However, the report states "2,000 team messages sent" which is correct -- this is 2,000 messages per team/workspace, NOT per user. The engagement report uses it correctly. After hitting 2,000 messages, 93% of those teams were still using Slack.
- For context: for a 50-person team, that is ~10 hours of messages; for a 10-person team, ~1 week of messages.
- Source: [First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/from-0-to-1b-slacks-founder-shares-their-epic-launch-strategy/)

### 4. "Push notification opt-in: social 48%, travel 70%"
- **Verdict: VERIFIED**
- Airship's 2025-2026 Global Benchmark Reports confirm iOS median opt-in of 48% for Social & Messaging and 70% for Travel & Transportation. The push notification report correctly attributes these and presents the full low/median/high ranges.
- Source: [Airship 2026 Benchmarks](https://www.airship.com/resources/mobile-app-push-notification-benchmarks-2026/)

### 5. "Average income $124,720/yr; median $85,000/yr"
- **Verdict: VERIFIED**
- Multiple 2025-2026 sources (DemandSage, TwoTicketsAnywhere, Atlys, RentRemote) consistently report average digital nomad income of $123,762-$124,720 and median of $85,000. The report's figures are accurate.
- Source: [DemandSage 2026](https://www.demandsage.com/digital-nomads-statistics/)

### 6. "56% male, 43% female"
- **Verdict: VERIFIED**
- MBO Partners 2025 report confirms 56% male, 43% female, 1% nonbinary among US digital nomads. The persona report rounds correctly.
- Source: [MBO Partners 2025](https://www.mbopartners.com/state-of-independence/digital-nomads/)

### 7. US iOS market share ~59.8%
- **Verdict: VERIFIED**
- DemandSage and TekRevol 2026 data confirm iOS holds 59.2-59.8% US market share vs. ~40% Android. The device preference analysis table is accurate.
- Source: [DemandSage 2026](https://www.demandsage.com/iphone-vs-android-users/)

## CORRECTED (Claim inaccurate or misattributed -- fix required)

### 8. "44% of nomads are American" -- Report says 43%
- **Verdict: CORRECTED -- both numbers circulate; the truth is in the range**
- The persona report says "43% of global" and "18.1 million." The push notification report and some 2025 sources cite 44%. The actual range across credible sources is 43-44%. MBO Partners counts 18.5 million US digital nomads. If the global total is 43M, that is 43%. If 42M, that is 44%.
- **Action:** Use "approximately 43-44%" rather than a precise single number. The persona report's 43% is within range; the memory file's 44% is also within range. Neither is wrong, but presenting a false precision of either is misleading.

### 9. "67% of nomads are single"
- **Verdict: CORRECTED -- actual figure is 65-66%**
- Multiple 2025-2026 sources (Passport-Photo.Online, CloudWards, TwoTicketsAnywhere) report 65-66% of digital nomads are single. The 67% figure does not appear in any primary source I could locate. The report inflated by 1-2 percentage points.
- **Action:** Correct to "approximately 65-66%."
- Sources: [Passport-Photo.Online 2026](https://passport-photo.online/blog/digital-nomad-statistics-and-trends/), [CloudWards 2025](https://www.cloudwards.net/digital-nomad-statistics/)

### 10. "45% of digital nomads report loneliness"
- **Verdict: CORRECTED -- figure is real but NOT from a peer-reviewed study**
- The 45% figure appears in multiple nomad statistics aggregator sites (ABrotherAbroad, ProjectUntethered) but none trace to a specific peer-reviewed study. The peer-reviewed study that exists -- Miguel et al. (2025), "Alone on the Road," published in Media, Culture and Society (Sage) -- is a qualitative study using 30 in-depth interviews. It does NOT produce a percentage. The 40% "frequent loneliness" figure from other aggregators is similarly unattributed to any single study.
- **Action:** Use "approximately 40-45% of nomads report loneliness (industry surveys)" and cite the Miguel et al. qualitative study separately for depth. Do NOT claim this is a "peer-reviewed" statistic -- the peer-reviewed research is qualitative, not quantitative.
- Source: [Miguel et al. 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/01634437241290087)

### 11. "Duolingo's 10-day streak is the retention inflection"
- **Verdict: CORRECTED -- the data supports 7 days more strongly than 10**
- Duolingo's own blog and Lenny's Newsletter (Jorge Mazal, Duolingo product team) identify **7 days** as the critical inflection point: "Learners who reach a streak of 7 are 2.4x more likely to continue." Duolingo's blog states the first 7 days are "most fragile" and that the share of DAU with 7+ day streaks tripled.
- The "10-day" figure appears in secondary sources (Plotline, Propel) but is NOT from Duolingo's own published data. Days 2-10 are described as the "danger zone" for churn, which may be where the confusion originated.
- **Action:** Correct to "7-day streak" as the retention inflection, with days 2-10 as the high-churn danger zone. The engagement report should cite Duolingo's blog directly.
- Sources: [Duolingo Blog - Streaks](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals/), [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)

### 12. "Read receipts = 61-74% higher 28-day retention"
- **Verdict: CORRECTED -- this is about in-app messaging, NOT read receipts**
- The 61-74% figure comes from Upland's Localytics study on **personalized in-app messaging**, not read receipts. The study found that brands using personalized in-app messages saw 61-74% 28-day retention vs. 49% for generic campaigns.
- This statistic does NOT appear in any of the four reports under review, but it was flagged for verification. If it appears elsewhere in x/pat documentation, it must be corrected. "Read receipts" and "in-app messaging" are completely different features.
- **Action:** If this claim exists anywhere in x/pat materials, reattribute it correctly to personalized in-app messaging (Localytics/Upland study).
- Source: [Localytics Blog](http://info.localytics.com/blog/in-app-messages-drive-higher-app-usage-and-engagement-benchmarks)

### 13. "Onboarding completion drops from 40% to 21% at 5 steps"
- **Verdict: CORRECTED -- the specific 40%-to-21% framing is not supported**
- The onboarding report does NOT actually make this exact claim. What it states is: (a) completion rates below 40% indicate significant friction, (b) each additional screen beyond 5 reduces completion by 8-12% (Mixpanel 2025), and (c) 6+ step flows see 18-24% completion vs. 31-42% for 3-4 step flows.
- The "40% to 21%" framing appears to be a misreading or conflation of two separate statistics: the 40% friction threshold and the 21% Day-1 retention benchmark. These are different metrics.
- **Action:** The onboarding report's actual claims are defensible. The 40%-to-21% framing should not be used as a standalone talking point.

## UNVERIFIABLE (No credible primary source found)

### 14. "Users who see empty feed churn at 67% within 24 hours"
- **Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE as stated**
- General 24-hour churn data shows approximately 70-75% of all users churn within 24 hours regardless of feed state (UXCam, CleverTap, AppsFlyer 2025-2026). The specific claim that "empty feed" causes 67% churn is not traceable to any published study.
- The onboarding report's actual language is "users who see an empty feed/map with no connections churn at 67% within 24 hours" -- this is presented without a source citation. The 67% figure is actually LOWER than the general 24-hour churn rate (~70-75%), making the claim paradoxically conservative but still unsourced.
- **Action:** Reframe as "general Day-1 churn across all apps is 70-75% (industry benchmarks); empty-state experiences likely exacerbate this." Remove the specific 67% figure or mark it as an estimate.

### 15. "Haptic feedback = 23% 30-day retention improvement"
- **Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE**
- This claim does NOT appear in any of the four reports under review. No published study linking haptic feedback to a specific 23% 30-day retention improvement could be found. Haptic feedback research exists (NeuroInsight found 50% engagement increase in mobile ads; University of Toronto found 12% response time improvement) but none produce a "23% 30-day retention" figure.
- **Action:** If this claim exists elsewhere in x/pat materials, remove it or replace with verified haptic feedback data.

### 16. "Apps combining streaks and milestones see 40-60% higher DAU"
- **Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE -- likely fabricated attribution**
- This claim appears on Plotline's blog and is attributed to "Forrester's 2024 research on mobile app retention." However, no Forrester report with this specific finding could be located in Forrester's public research library. The Plotline blog post is not a peer-reviewed or industry-standard source, and the Forrester attribution may be invented or refer to a non-public client report.
- **Action:** Downgrade to "industry estimates suggest" and remove the Forrester attribution. The directional claim (gamification improves DAU) is well-supported, but the specific 40-60% figure lacks a credible primary source.

### 17. "5-word notification rule (94% engagement lift)"
- **Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIABLE -- attribution chain is weak**
- The claim that "messages containing 7 words or fewer generated a 94% engagement lift over those exceeding 15 words" with "5 words" as the optimal length is attributed to "OneSignal's analysis of 4.7 billion push notifications." However, the only source for this is Amra & Elma's marketing statistics compilation (March 2026), not a direct OneSignal publication. OneSignal's own 2026 Best Practices guide and their published research reports do not contain this specific statistic.
- OneSignal DID publish a 2022 analysis of 118 billion push notifications with length-related insights, but the specific "94% lift" and "5 words" figures do not appear in that original report.
- **Action:** Retain the directional insight (shorter notifications perform better -- this is well-established) but remove the specific "94% lift" figure and "5-word rule" unless a direct OneSignal publication can be located.

## NEW DATA (Findings that should be added to x/pat research)

### A. Duolingo's CURR Methodology (More Precise Than Reported)
- Duolingo increased CURR by 21% over four years, which 4.5x'd DAU. Monthly churn declined from 47% (mid-2020) to 37% (early 2023). The 2% monthly CURR improvement target stated in the engagement report is accurate and comes from Duolingo's own team via Lenny's Newsletter.
- Duolingo surpassed 50M DAU in Q3 2025 (36% YoY growth), then accelerated to 51% DAU growth in Q4 2025. They target 100M DAU by 2028. The engagement report correctly cites these figures.

### B. Digital Nomad Ethnicity Data Needs Updating
- The persona report states "67% white, 21% African American, 11% Hispanic, 6% Asian" -- this totals 105%, which is mathematically impossible unless multi-racial identification is allowed.
- MBO Partners 2025 data shows: 66% white, 27% African American (UP from 21% in 2023), 8% Hispanic, 4% Asian, 2% other. The African American share has been rising significantly year-over-year. The persona report uses outdated 2023 data for some categories while mixing in 2025 data for others.
- **Action:** Update to 2025 MBO Partners figures and note that percentages may exceed 100% if multi-racial identification is permitted, or normalize to 100%.

### C. Slack's Metric Is Team-Level, Not User-Level
- The engagement report correctly states "2,000 team messages sent" but the aha moment comparison table just says "2,000 team messages sent" without emphasizing this is a team/workspace metric, not a per-user metric. This distinction matters enormously for x/pat's activation metric design. x/pat's proposed "3 spots + 1 chat message" is a per-user metric, which is the correct framing for a consumer social app (like Facebook's metric) rather than a team productivity tool (like Slack's metric).

### D. MBO Partners Is the Gold Standard Source
- The MBO Partners "State of Independence" report is the most methodologically rigorous annual survey of US digital nomads (large sample, consistent methodology, published since 2019). Many of the statistics floating around nomad blogs ultimately trace back to MBO Partners data. The research team should cite MBO Partners directly rather than aggregator blogs whenever possible.

### E. The "Slowmad" Trend Has Quantifiable Drivers
- Airfares up 7.1% YoY (cited in persona report) is a contributing factor, but the bigger driver is employer policy: MBO Partners 2025 finds that digital nomads now comprise 12% of the US workforce, with increasing employer acceptance of location-independent work. This legitimization enables longer stays. The persona report should add employer policy acceptance as a primary driver alongside cost.

The research team's demographic data is generally solid and well-sourced. The engagement psychology statistics are where quality drops -- several figures are taken from marketing blog aggregators rather than primary sources, and at least two attributions (Forrester gamification study, OneSignal 5-word rule) appear to be fabricated or unverifiable citations from secondary content farms. The Duolingo 10-day vs. 7-day error is the most consequential correction because it directly affects x/pat's streak system design target.

---

## Recommended Actions

1. **Correct Duolingo streak target from 10 days to 7 days** across all product documents and sprint specs
2. **Remove or downgrade the four UNVERIFIABLE statistics** -- replace with directionally correct statements that cite verifiable benchmarks
3. **Fix the ethnicity data** to 2025 MBO Partners figures and address the >100% total
4. **Standardize source hierarchy**: MBO Partners > Duolingo investor filings/blog > Airship benchmarks > peer-reviewed journals > industry blogs > marketing aggregator sites. Never cite aggregators when primary data is available.
5. **Reattribute the 61-74% retention figure** if it appears anywhere in product specs -- it is about personalized in-app messaging, not read receipts
6. **Add "approximately" qualifiers** to demographic percentages that vary by 1-3% across sources (nomad population, US share, single percentage)
